My Full Review

What I Wrote After Finishing It

My notebook thoughts, rewritten for the screen — spoilers and all.

The Other Side of Midnight was a book that kept making me change how I felt about its characters.

The story revolves heavily around Noelle, Catherine, and Larry, and the complicated relationships between them. What begins with love gradually becomes tangled in betrayal, obsession, revenge, and consequences.

Noelle was especially interesting to me because of the way her character develops. She begins from a place of love and vulnerability, but betrayal changes her. Her pain eventually becomes tied to revenge, and that transformation drives a major part of the story.

Catherine, on the other hand, was the character I found myself arguing with the most while reading.

There were moments when I understood her situation and felt sympathy for her. But there were also many moments when I wanted her to see what was happening around her and make completely different choices.

And then there is Larry.

His actions connect so much of the emotional damage in the story. The way he moves between relationships and the decisions he makes create consequences for both women, and I found myself increasingly frustrated with him as the plot unfolded.

What made the book engaging for me was that the characters were not simple. I could dislike something one of them did while still understanding why they felt the way they did.

The story also made me think about revenge. Noelle's desire for revenge becomes such a powerful force that it begins shaping her identity and her future.

In that sense, revenge becomes another connection to the betrayal itself. Even while trying to punish the person who hurt her, she remains emotionally tied to what happened.

Overall, The Other Side of Midnight was dramatic, intense, messy, and very difficult to read without having opinions.

I didn't simply finish the story — I spent a good portion of it mentally arguing with the characters, changing sides, questioning their decisions, and wondering how everything would finally end.
