My Full Review

What I Wrote After Finishing It

My original notes, rewritten from notebook to screen.

Atomic Habits was a book I had heard about for a long time, especially through recommendations from others. Once I finally read it, I understood why so many people speak highly of it.

One of the most important ideas I took from the book was the difference between outcomes, processes, and identity. Outcomes are what we get, processes are what we do, and identity is what we believe about ourselves.

What really stood out to me was the idea that people often focus too much on what they want to achieve. The book instead encourages us to focus on the kind of person we want to become.

For example, saying “I am trying to stop smoking” still connects the habit to the old identity, while saying “I am not a smoker” reflects a deeper identity shift.

Another important concept was the process of building habits through four stages: cue, craving, response, and reward. The cue initiates the behaviour, craving creates motivation, response is the action, and reward is the result that reinforces it.

To create a good habit, the book suggests making it obvious, attractive, easy, and satisfying. To break a bad habit, the opposite approach can be used: make it invisible, unattractive, difficult, and unsatisfying.

I also liked the idea of habit stacking — attaching a new behaviour to something you already do regularly. That makes the new habit easier to remember and more natural to repeat.

Overall, this book made me think less about dramatic transformation and more about the power of small repeated actions. The message that stayed with me most is simple: decide who you want to become, then prove it to yourself through small wins.
