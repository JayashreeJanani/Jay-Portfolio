My Full Review

What I Wrote After Finishing It

My original thoughts, rewritten from notebook to screen.

Homecoming was another recommendation that came to me, and I really liked the way the story revolves around women, family, trauma, choices, and the past.

There are male characters in the story, of course, but the emotional centre of the book felt strongly connected to the women and the decisions that shaped their lives.

I especially connected with Jess. Her uncertainty about her career and her life after that felt very relatable to me. I liked the confusion in her character, because it made her feel more realistic rather than perfectly certain about everything.

The main mystery surrounding the Summers family was what kept pulling me forward. At first, the book moves slowly, but as the story develops, the different pieces begin to connect and the final part brings several twists that completely reshape the way you understand what came before.

One thing I particularly liked was the way the novel explores how people carry memories, secrets, and family history across generations. The past is never really gone — it continues to influence the present.

There were also several emotional moments that stayed with me. The story made me think about how trauma affects not only one person, but an entire family and the decisions people make later in life.

Most importantly, the book gave me a new way to think about home. Home was not simply the physical place where someone lived. It became a feeling — a sense of belonging, completeness, memory, and connection.

Overall, I found Homecoming emotional, mysterious, and rewarding. It requires patience at the beginning, but the story becomes much more powerful as the different threads come together.
